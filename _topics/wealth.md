in_short: |
  Wealth is what households own (their home, savings, shares, a business) minus what they owe. It is distributed far more unequally than income. Wealth matters because it is a safety net against job loss or illness, a source of opportunity (to study, to start a business, to help one's children), and because it is passed on across generations.

  Wealth is also hard to measure. The wealthiest households are rarely reached by surveys and tend to under-report; surveys are infrequent; and some assets, such as private businesses or holdings abroad, are difficult to value. This topic uses new data sources to measure wealth and household financial behaviour more accurately and more often.

## Why it matters for Europe

Wealth shapes how households respond to crises and to policy. Interest rate decisions, taxation, housing policy and financial regulation all affect wealth, and their effects depend on who owns what. Better wealth data are also essential for fairness between generations, as access to home ownership and inheritance increasingly determine young people's prospects.

## What we study

- **Wealth at the top:** correcting for under-reporting and for wealthy households missing from surveys.
- **New data:** tax records, property registers, bank transactions and card payments, used alongside surveys and under strict privacy rules.
- **Debt and credit:** who borrows, at what cost and with what risk.
- **Saving and investment:** how households invest, and why some earn much higher returns on their wealth than others.
- **Housing wealth:** the role of home ownership and house prices in wealth inequality.
- **Inflation and wealth:** who gains and who loses when prices rise.
- **Timely wealth statistics:** linking survey data to national accounts to estimate the distribution of wealth every quarter.

## Methods and data

We combine the Household Finance and Consumption Survey, the Distributional Wealth Accounts of the European Central Bank and national surveys with administrative and transaction data. Statistical methods correct for missing and under-reported wealth; simulation tools estimate how policies would change the distribution of wealth.
