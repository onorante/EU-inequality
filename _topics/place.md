  A second dimension is social capital: the networks, trust and meeting places that hold communities together. Libraries, sports clubs, community centres, cafés and squares are where people meet across age, income and background. When these places disappear, people may feel left behind, with consequences for well-being, participation and trust in institutions. This topic measures how opportunities and social ties are distributed across Europe, down to the local level.

## Why it matters for Europe

Cohesion policy, rural development and the Single Market all aim at a Europe where opportunities are available everywhere. Local measures of opportunity and social capital help target investment, assess whether policies reach the places that need them, and understand the roots of discontent and disengagement.

## What we study

- **Where opportunity is built:** how local labour markets, housing and amenities shape people's decisions to work, save and invest, using models of individual behaviour linked to local conditions.
- **Spillovers between places:** how shocks spread across regional borders.
- **Social infrastructure:** the availability and accessibility of meeting places across the EU, and how they change over time.
- **Trust and cohesion:** how social capital is distributed across regions and communities.
- **Feeling left behind:** the gap between perceived and measured inequality, and its links with electoral participation and political discontent.
- **Freedom to stay:** depopulation, brain drain, and places stuck in low-growth trajectories.

## Methods and data

We use geographic data (including OpenStreetMap and administrative registers), data on social networks and perceptions, and dynamic computational models of individual decisions. The analysis is carried out at regional and local level, down to municipalities where data allow, using the NUTS regions and the geographic data of Eurostat's GISCO service.
