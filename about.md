## About this site

EU Inequality brings together research on how income, wealth and opportunity are shared across the people and regions of the European Union, and on better ways to measure them, so that policy can be judged by who it reaches.

It is an independent research website. It is not an official website of the European Union or of any EU institution, and the views expressed are those of the authors.

## Why this work is needed

1. **Data arrive late and rarely.** The main European survey of household wealth, the Household Finance and Consumption Survey, has so far been carried out every three to four years (2010, 2014, 2017 and 2021), and its results are published with a further delay. By the time a full picture is available, the economy has often changed: a pandemic, an energy crisis, a surge in inflation.
2. **Income is only part of the picture.** Housing, energy, health, education, digital access, transport and free time all shape living standards. Several of these dimensions are not measured regularly, or not in enough detail to compare regions and groups.
3. **Shocks hit people unevenly.** Crises and policy decisions affect households differently depending on their income, wealth, debt, job and place of residence. Many tools used for policy analysis still look mainly at averages and cannot show who is most exposed.
4. **Place matters.** Opportunities increasingly concentrate in some areas, while others lose jobs, services and young people. The European debate on the "freedom to stay", a notion put forward in Enrico Letta's 2024 report on the Single Market, holds that moving away should be a choice, not a necessity. Measuring opportunity across places is a precondition for acting on it.

## Our approach

We combine three ingredients:

- **New data:** administrative records, tax and property registers, bank and payment data, text and geographic data, used alongside traditional surveys.
- **New methods:** artificial intelligence, non-linear statistics, and economic models that represent many different households rather than one average household.
- **New measures:** indicators of inequality across several dimensions, at European, national and regional level, that can be updated over time.

The work is organised in [five research topics]({{ '/topics/' | relative_url }}).

## Context: a European Parliament Preparatory Action

Much of the research presented here is connected to the Preparatory Action "New methodologies for assessing inequalities". Preparatory Actions are an instrument through which the European Parliament finances, within the EU budget, new initiatives that test ideas and prepare possible future EU action. This one, carried out at the Joint Research Centre of the European Commission, asks how Europe can measure inequality in a way that is more complete, more timely and more useful for policy.

Research teams from universities and research institutes, selected through an open call, contribute to the research topics, and a panel of international experts advises on methods and reviews results. The expected results include:

- a handbook, *New methodologies for assessing inequalities*, presenting new data and measurement approaches, the links between inequality and economic and social outcomes, and recommendations for policy;
- a collection of measures of inequality across different dimensions, at EU, national and detailed regional level, as a basis for future monitoring;
- three events bringing together policymakers and researchers: a launch, an academic workshop and a closing event;
- seminars and courses, in person and online, on new methods to measure inequality.

## Our principles

- **Rigorous:** methods are documented and results are open to scientific review.
- **Open:** publications are open access; data and code are shared whenever licences and privacy rules allow.
- **Clear:** every result is also explained for non-specialists, together with the policy question it answers.
- **European:** measures are comparable across Member States and, where possible, down to regions and municipalities.
