How unequal is Europe, and how do we know?

We study how income, wealth and opportunity are shared across the people and regions of the European Union, and we develop better ways to measure it, so that policy can be judged by who it reaches.

What we measure

Inequality is more than a single number. We look at the different ways people end up ahead or behind, and at how these gaps add up.

Income
Earnings, transfers and taxes: who gains and who loses from the way income is earned and redistributed.
Wealth
Housing, savings, pensions and debt. Wealth is far more concentrated than income, and harder to observe.
Places
Gaps between and within regions, down to the local level, and why some areas fall behind while others pull ahead.
Childhood
How the circumstances children grow up in shape their chances later in life.
Consumption and climate
Whose footprint is largest, and who bears the cost of the green transition.

How we measure it

Averages hide the people at the edges. Our methods follow whole distributions and how they change over time, across places and in response to policy.

Distributional econometrics
Models of entire distributions rather than means, and of how they respond to economic shocks.
Household microdata
European surveys such as EU-SILC and the Household Finance and Consumption Survey, combined with administrative and new data sources.
Models with many households
Macroeconomic models in which households differ in income and wealth, to trace how monetary and fiscal policy reach each of them.
Spatial models
How geography shapes inequality, and how shocks spread across regional borders.
