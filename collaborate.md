No single institution can measure inequality alone. The data are scattered across statistical offices, central banks, administrations and private organisations; the methods are developed in many universities; and the questions come from policymakers and citizens.

## Who we would like to hear from

- **Researchers** working on the measurement of inequality, distributional methods, household finance, macroeconomic modelling or spatial analysis.
- **Data holders:** statistical offices, central banks, tax and social security administrations, and private organisations whose data could improve the measurement of inequality, always under strict privacy and confidentiality rules.
- **Policymakers and analysts** in European, national and regional institutions who need better evidence on inequality.
- **Civil society, journalists and educators** who help bring evidence on inequality to a wider public.

## How to collaborate

- **Present your work** in the [seminar series]({{ '/events/' | relative_url }}).
- **Share or connect data** through partnerships that respect confidentiality.
- **Write with us:** joint papers and policy briefs.
- **Take part** in our workshops and calls for papers.
- **Tell us your questions:** which questions about inequality should research address?

## What you can expect

Visibility for your work in a European network, dialogue with policymakers, joint publications, and access to our methods and results.
